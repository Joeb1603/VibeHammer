## D-083: Teamwork: the bots fight as an army (M78–M83; part 0, M78: measuring it and the M77 regression switch; part 1, M79: the army plan; part 2, M80: Normal moves as a group; part 3, M81: focus fire and joint charges; part 4, M82: Hard on the army plan; part 5, M83: tuning and the final numbers)
**Decided:** 2026-11-10 to 2026-11-11 (the pre-approved M78–M83 batch, PLAN.md "Teamwork"; the conservative readings
are marked *conservative*). Bot and arena only throughout: no rule, core, state, action, event or file format change.

**Summary.** Before the batch Normal scored each unit's move alone against its closest enemy unit and Hard valued
every pair of units as an independent duel, so on the rosters most units held back and the monsters arrived one at a
time. The batch measures teamwork in the arena (part 0), gives each side an **army plan** (part 1: per enemy unit, the
smallest group of two or three of our units whose joint exchange pays where each alone would not), and makes the bots
play it: a unit that follows its group lines up on the group's target, counts its partners' volleys and shares the
target's reply, and waits at the edge of reach for them (part 2); shooting concentrates and finishes wounded units,
partners join a charge (part 3); Hard's army value leads each group member's queue with the group's target and lets the
group wipe it at its combined pace (part 4); and a unit with no group closes in beyond the horizon unless its closest
enemy unit outreaches it, with the follow gate lowered to 0.1 (part 5). It amends **D-066** (the line follows the group's
focus, not only the closest enemy unit; the charged value counts partners), **D-054**'s shooting and charge choices
(M41, M42: focus fire, the finishing bonus, the split margin, the charge order), **D-067** (the queues and a group
target's P(wiped)), **D-077** (the move score through D-066; the approach terms) and **D-078** (part 11's
`move_search_units`, measured again and kept at 7). V1, both bot scenarios and Easy play exactly as before;
`Generation::M77` (`normal-m77` / `hard-m77`) plays the pre-batch bots. The final numbers and the targets left open
are in part 5.

Part 0 is bot and arena only, and changes no play anywhere: no rule, core, state, replay or JSON change.
- **Part 0 (M78): the arena's teamwork statistics** (`src/ai/arena.{hpp,cpp}`). `play_bot_session` feeds a
  `TeamworkCounter` the initial state and then, after every accepted action, that action's events and the state right
  after it. To make that possible the client's automatic actions are now counted one at a time (run_auto_actions with
  a submit callback that is the default `session.submit` followed by the count; the same actions in the same order).
  The per-game result is `GameRecord::teamwork` (`TeamworkStats`, per side), summed in `ArenaResult::teamwork`, and
  printed by `format_arena` as three new lines, per game. **The `--per-game` line is unchanged** (the identity set
  parses it). Definitions, per side:
  - **Gang-ups:** taken at the start of each Fight phase (the state after the action whose events hold
    `PhaseStarted{Fight}`: the positions the charges left, before any pile-in), in both players' turns. A gang-up is
    an enemy unit with models that two or more of our units are engaged with (03.04, `engaged_enemy_units`); the
    count is of (Fight phase, enemy unit) pairs. Also the distinct enemy units ever ganged up on in the game.
    *Conservative:* the start of the phase, not each fight activation (one clear moment, unaffected by the order the
    fights are picked in); a Fight phase the engine ends inside the action that starts it has no engaged unit, so it
    counts nothing.
  - **Lone engagements:** at the same moment, our units engaged with at least one enemy unit that no other unit of ours
    is within 12" of (edge to edge, `unit_distance`, `within`). An engaged unit is within 2", so "engaged with it or
    within 12" of it" is one test. Each unit counts once per Fight phase, however many enemy units it fights.
  - **Shooting spread:** for each `ShootingStarted`, the distinct target units of its selections (the mean per Shoot);
    and for each shooting phase in which a side made a weapon selection, the share of that side's selections at its
    most-selected target unit (the mean over those phases). *Conservative:* a selection is one model's weapon profile
    at one target (as `ShootingStarted` lists them), not attack dice, so the share needs no dice and doesn't move with
    random attacks or Blast. A phase is closed by any `PhaseStarted` / `PhaseEnded` event, or the game's end.
  - **Contact:** after the initial state and every accepted action: the units ever on the battlefield with models,
    and those of them that never came within 12" of an enemy unit with models (a unit destroyed before it did counts;
    a unit never set up, D-081, isn't on the battlefield and doesn't count); and the battle round in which a side first
    had an engaged unit (the mean over the games with one; the games without are counted). An engagement is both
    sides', so the first-engagement round is the same for both sides of a game.
  - **The summary lines** (format_arena, after the pile-in line): `teamwork (M78), per game: gang-ups A x / B y
    (distinct enemy units ...); lone engagements ...`, `shooting spread (M78): distinct targets per shoot ...;
    top-target share of a shooting phase's weapon selections ...` and `contact (M78), per game: units never within 12"
    of an enemy unit A x of n / B y of m; first engagement round A r (k games without) / B ...`.
  - **Cost:** a unit-distance check per unit after every action, skipped once a unit has been within 12" (the
    engagement test runs until the side's first engagement); decision times are unaffected (they are timed around
    `decide` only).

- **Part 1 (M79): the army plan** (`src/ai/normal/army_plan.{hpp,cpp}`; designed first and measured with a scratch
  prototype, M79 being plan mode, then reviewed: the no-op case below was narrowed, the rest accepted as designed; the
  implementation gives the prototype's plans exactly, at 1,317 Move Units decisions of 5 rosters and 20 random-army
  games). `ArmyPlan army_plan(OddsCache&, const GameState&, Side, Generation)` gives each of
  `side`'s units with models a **focus** (an enemy unit) and its **partners** (our units with the same focus). It is a
  pure, deterministic function of the state (the bots stay stateless: maps keyed by unit id, everything in unit order,
  ties to unit order), recomputed by each decision that uses it. Nothing reads it in M79, so no play changes; M80–M82
  do. Bot only: no rule, core, state, action, event, replay or JSON change.
  - **Turns.** Our turn 0 is the current turn if it is ours, else our next one; K is the number of our turns left in the
    game (0 after the game's end). What a unit still has of turn 0:
    - everything (move, shooting, charge) in the opponent's turn, before our Movement phase, or while it isn't yet
      selected to move. A unit in the middle of its move counts as not moved, its models taken at the move's start (as
      MoveScoreContext does), so a replan from mid-move gets the same plan;
    - once it has moved: no move; shooting if it hasn't fallen back or shot yet (until the Shooting phase ends); a
      charge if it hasn't advanced, fallen back or declared yet (until the Charge phase ends);
    - from the Fight phase on: nothing new (an engaged unit still fights).
  - **Reach** (per pair (our unit u, enemy unit e); no odds needed):
    - d0 is the gap now, edge to edge (unit_distance). T(k) is the travel by our turn k's Normal move: k(M + A) + M,
      where A = 3.5" (09.06's D6 mean: a unit advances in the turns before it attacks; 0 when M is 0). With no move
      left in turn 0, T(0) = 0 and T(k) = (k − 1)(M + A) + M.
    - Shooting arrival ts: the first k < K in which it may shoot and d0 − T(k) is within its longest usable range R
      (ThreatRanges::max_range_mm; for k = 0 that is shooting_reach_mm).
    - Charge arrival tc: the first k < K in which it may charge and the 2D6 covers max(d0 − T(k), 2") with P ≥ 0.5
      (p_charge_roll_covers: within 7" after the move).
    - Engaged with e now: arrival 0. With neither: no arrival, so u is not a candidate for e.
  - **Modes,** one per pair: the one whose lone exchange (below) is better (a tie goes to Shoot).
    - *Shoot:* from ts it shoots in each of our turns from min(d0, R). It closes only to its range, so the reply meets
      it there. [HEAVY] applies when it needn't move (d0 ≤ R) or after its first volley. Before ts it advances (gap
      d0 − T(k) − A).
    - *Charge:* it advances until tc. At tc: a Normal move, its shooting from d0 − T(tc) if in range, then the charge,
      with p = P(the roll covers). From then on it is engaged (certain: p ≥ 0.5 by tc's definition).
    - *Engaged:* engaged with e now.
    - While engaged, each of our turns has its close-quarters shooting (10.06) and its fight.
  - **The exchange** of a group G (1 to 3 of our units, in unit order) with one target e. It is a MultiExchange with the
    roles turned round: one defender (e) and several attackers (D-066's class, unchanged).
    - Each member's volley on e is scaled by what is left of the member, e's volley on a member by what is left of e,
      and each loss is capped at what is left of its target.
    - *Strengths* are what is left of a unit: its models' wounds left over their W (model equivalents), as unit_share.
      So a wounded monster is worth less and easier to finish. This is the plan's own choice: Normal's ScoreLine counts
      models, which counted a Hive Tyrant at 3 wounds as whole.
    - *Volleys* are the existing rates: melee_share, close-quarters ranged_share, and ranged_share in next_turn_shot's
      context (who is engaged now: 04.02, 10.06, 17.03; [HAZARDOUS] priced, D-077). The engagements are found once per
      call, and the rates are cached per call by (attacker, target, kind, range band, [HEAVY]). The OddsCache caches
      only per-die odds and lives for one decision, so nothing is kept between calls.
    - *Sequence:* our turns j = 0, 1, … until three of our turns from the group's first contact t0 (its earliest
      arrival) are played, or the game ends:
      - **our turn j:** every member shoots (its mode) and the chargers at tc charge. In the Fight phase every member
        engaged with e strikes it first (chargers have Fights First, 11.04; already engaged, the active player selects
        first, 12.04; the first charge counts × p). Then e strikes back at one of them.
      - **e's turn after our turn j** (if the enemy has one before our third volley turn):
        - engaged with any member: e fights one of them (close-quarters shooting and its fight), then every engaged
          member strikes back;
        - otherwise e makes its reply worst for us. The options, for each member: shoot it (if its gap is within e's M
          plus longest range: from min(gap, R_e), [HEAVY] within R_e), or shoot it and charge it (P(charge after a
          Normal move) × e's melee, and the member strikes back × the same P). A charge at P ≥ 0.5 leaves them engaged.
          A turn with no charge brings e its M closer, for its own later reach: it closes on the group.
      - e's replies before t0 count too: the members approaching into its reach. This is D-067's "who reaches whom
        when", on both sides.
    - *Value:* e's losses minus the members' losses (positive: we win it).
    - *Who takes the reply:* the member the target's choice hurts most (the largest swing in the exchange's value,
      strike-back included); a tie goes to unit order. It is not the member closest to the target (D-010). The reply
      is the enemy player's choice, so the plan assumes the worst for us, as hold_line does; it also doesn't depend on
      the formation a move will choose. The plan reports the member of the first reply (`reply_to`). M80's shared-reply
      rule (inside the group, after a move) is the move score's own.
    - *Horizon:* three of our volleys and the target's two replies between them, cut at the game's end. The brief's
      example was two (Normal's own horizon), but with two the target's first strike is never repaid: two Legionaries
      units 18" from a Screamer-killer come out −0.08 with two volleys and +0.06 with three.
  - **Lone values and what a unit gets apart.**
    - lone(u, e) is the exchange of {u} with e.
    - What u gets apart is alone(u, e) = max(0, lone(u, e), lone(u, its closest enemy unit)). 0 means it holds back,
      as today's Normal does when it would lose; today's line is the one with its closest enemy unit. A unit engaged
      with e gets lone(u, e) (it can't hold back).
    - The surplus of a group is gain(G, e) = value(G, e) − Σ alone(u, e).
    - A group pays when it has at least 2 members, value > 0.05 and gain > 0.05 (kMinValue, kMinGain: 5% of a unit of
      the mean points; on Strike, 5% of a unit). A single unit never pays: its gain is never positive.
    - PLAN.md's "beats every member's best value alone" is read as the sum of the members' alternatives, not the
      largest one: units outside a group still do their best apart, so a group pays only for its surplus. This is
      stricter (*conservative*).
  - **Assignment (greedy).**
    - Candidates for a target e:
      - the "seeds": our units engaged with e whose closest enemy unit it is. They are always members;
      - up to 4 unassigned, unengaged units that reach e soonest (min(ts, tc), then the gap, then unit order), with an
        arrival before K.
      - An engaged unit is a candidate only for the unit it is engaged with.
    - The group for e: of the paying subsets of the candidates (always with the seeds; at most 3 members, or the seeds
      alone if there are more), the best by gain per risk among those of the smallest size. Risk is the members'
      strengths summed. A bigger size wins only if its best group gains 0.05 more than the smaller size's: each extra
      member must pay its way.
    - Repeat: of the targets left, the one whose group has the best gain per risk takes it (a tie goes to the target's
      unit order, then the members'). Its members leave the pool. Stop when no group pays.
    - An unassigned unit keeps today's line: its focus is its closest enemy unit, with no partners. An engaged unit's
      closest enemy unit is one it is engaged with (within 2"), so that is its focus.
    - *Amends the PLAN.md outline*, which ranked targets by joint value per points at risk and took each target's
      smallest paying group. Measured with the prototype (the plan at each turn's first MoveUnits decision, 20 rosters
      games and 40 random-army games, Normal vs Normal; strengths still counted by models then):
      - ranking by gain per risk gives more surplus per plan than joint value per risk: 0.233 against 0.215 (rosters)
        and 0.142 against 0.123 (random armies);
      - letting a third member join when it adds 0.05 gives more than the smallest group: 0.233 against 0.194, and
        0.142 against 0.128.
    - Margins: 0.02 lets in noise groups (18% / 20% more units with partners for 3% / 4% more surplus; the 10th
      percentile of gains falls to 0.04); 0.1 drops real ones (15% / 27% fewer units with partners, 3% / 9% less
      surplus). Candidates per target: 3 found 18% / 15% fewer units with partners; 5 found 11% / 2% more (14% / 8% more
      surplus) for a rosters p95 of 2.52 ms against 1.59.
  - **No-ops.** In these cases every unit's focus is its closest enemy unit, with no partners, and `ArmyPlan::active`
    is false:
    - `pre_teamwork(generation)` (M55, M77);
    - fewer than two of **our** units with models (one unit a side, or our last unit: no partners are possible). An
      enemy side down to its last unit still gets a plan: ganging up on it (the last monster, say) is what the plan is
      for. The design first made either side's last unit a no-op (*conservative*); the review narrowed it to ours. The
      one-unit scenarios are 1v1, so they are no-ops either way;
    - no turn left (K = 0);
    - before the battle (NotStarted, Deploy).
  - **Points or shares.** Every value is a unit_share: weighted by points under the points rule with several datasheets
    (D-077, D-080), plain shares on Strike, V1 and a battle of one datasheet a side. Nothing else depends on the result
    rule. Under D-007 a side that loses the exchange plays for the draw in the move score (D-066); whether such a unit
    should still follow its group is for M80 to decide and measure.
  - **Cost** per call:
    - |ours| × |theirs| reaches (gaps and arrivals, no odds);
    - lone exchanges for at most 4 candidates per target and for each unit's closest enemy unit (2 modes each, at most
      3 volleys);
    - at most 10 subsets per target per greedy round, which need no new odds.
    - The odds (expected_outcome) are the cost: at most about 6 per pair evaluated, and at most 333 in any measured
      call.
    - Prototype, Release, at every decision of 10 rosters games and 40 random-army games (5,592 and 14,101 calls):
      rosters mean 0.77 ms, p95 1.59, max 2.75; random armies 0.55 / 1.54 / 4.96. The largest random battle (7 v 7,
      seed 63): p95 1.92, max 3.11.
    - The implementation (flat per-call tables, the engagements found once), the same calls: rosters mean 0.66 ms, p95
      1.34, max 2.95; random armies 0.45 / 1.27 / 3.34. One earlier run's random-army maximum, 13.2 ms, did not recur in
      the rerun (a slow-call check, timing each slow call five more times, found none): a scheduling outlier.
    - Normal's decision p95 is about 9–11 ms, so a consumer computes the plan once per decision: M80 once per MoveUnits
      decision (never per candidate move), M82 once per Hard decision at the root (never per evaluated state). The
      implementation must stay within 2 ms p95 and 5 ms max per call, checked by a printed timing test.

- **Part 2 (M80): Normal moves as a group** (`src/ai/normal/move_score.{hpp,cpp}`, `movement.{hpp,cpp}`; Hard through
  Normal's move plans, `hard_bot.cpp`). Bot only: no rule, core, state, action, event or JSON change. Amends D-066 (the
  line follows the closest enemy unit, except for a unit that follows its group) and, through it, D-077's move score.
  The first milestone that changes play: on the rosters and custom battles; V1, the bot scenarios, Strike (every pairing,

  - **The plan once per decision.** `decide_move_units` computes `army_plan` once (Hard's `hard_move_units` likewise,
    and passes it to Normal's answer), and every `MoveScoreContext` of that decision (Remain Stationary, the Normal
    move's plan, each Advance roll, the escape) reads it: the plan at the move's start is the plan at Move Units (the
    moving unit counts as not moved, part 1). `continue_unit_move` and `hard_plan_unit_move` compute it once when called
    alone. A context given no plan computes its own (`plan == nullptr`); a pre-teamwork generation (M55, M77) reads none.
  - **Which units follow a group.** A unit follows its group when (*conservative*, measured below):
    - it has partners in the plan and its focus has models;
    - the group is a clear surplus: its gain is at least `MoveWeights::follow` (0.2) of its risk (what its members put
      at stake, part 1). Two full Legionaries units on a Screamer-killer (+0.13 to +0.22 for 2.55 at risk) don't clear
      it; a wounded Hive Tyrant and Genestealers (+0.42 for 0.98) do;
    - under D-007 (no points rule) it doesn't lose its exchange with the focus (its best attacks at half range and in
      melee against the focus's, as `lean_`): a losing unit plays for the draw (D-066) and keeps its own line. On Strike
      this and the gate leave every unit as it was (the Termagant groups of part 1 are 0.1 of their risk), so Strike
      plays exactly as before; the brief's question ("should a unit that plays for the draw still follow its group?")
      is answered no, *conservative*, and couldn't be told apart on Strike (D-007 on or off: the same 200 games).
    - Every other unit scores exactly as before: nothing is added to its sum (tested: the same plan as `Generation::M77`).
  - **The line.** While some partner reaches the focus by our next turn (its arrival in the plan at most 1: "synced"),
    the line follows the focus, and the closest enemy unit, if another, is one of D-066's others (marginal exposure
    under the points rule, as any other). Until then the unit keeps its closest enemy unit's line, and only the approach
    and cohesion terms and the candidates follow the focus.

    their mode in the plan: a shooter's from min(gap, its longest range) (`next_turn_shot`); a charger's fight at the
    odds of covering the gap left after its moves (`p_charge_roll_covers`), then engaged (close-quarters shooting and
    its fight); an engaged member's close-quarters shooting and fight. This turn's land after our shooting, our next
    turn's at its start (and in the engaged lines' next turns). They take the focus down on the line (its replies are
    scaled by what is left of it, and our later hits are capped at it) but are not this unit's value: the partners hit
    the focus whatever this move does.
  - **Shared reply.** The focus attacks one unit a phase (D-010). When this unit ends in its reach (its Move plus its
    longest range, or a charge after a Normal move) the focus's reply counts on it with the share 1/k, k = 1 + the synced
    partners in its reach where they stand; the rest of the time it answers a partner, and only the other enemy units
    reply to us (`hold_line(..., reply_elsewhere)`). The brief's "the focus's preferred target in the group, as the plan
    decides who takes the reply" is read as an even split (measured best of three, below).

    partners (`M80_Normal.ALoneUnitWaitsOutsideTheMonstersChargeReach`). A partner 20" back never forms a group with it

      moves. So the enemy model is D-067's robust one (each enemy unit attacks the closest of ours first), and the plan
      the value reads is the one the deciding side will itself play. The enemy's plan did predict results better (MSE of V

    - *The root's, not each valued state's:* every option of a decision is valued against the same groups (within one
      Movement phase only 57-63% of an earlier mover's partners are still its partners when it moves, M80; a plan per
      valued state would make a candidate's value jump where it changes the plan); it costs nothing measurable; and it is
      M79's design note ("once per Hard decision at the root, never per evaluated state"). The per-state alternative was
      built and measured (below): about 27% more decision time, and weaker.
  - **The queue rule.** A unit of ours is in a group at a valued state when our plan gives it partners, the group's target
    has models there and at least two of the group's members (it among them) have models there. Then the target leads its
    queue (after the charged pair's target in a charge's fight value), unless it is engaged with an enemy unit and not with
    the target (an engaged unit deals with what it fights first, its main enemy); every other enemy unit follows by
    distance, as before. Every other unit's queue is distance alone, as before (its sort key is its gap: the same order, to
    the bit). In code the queue entry keeps its gap (arrival and travel read it) and gets a separate sort key.
  - **The timeline** is D-067's, the same arithmetic; it now also records the expected wounds each attacker takes from
    each target (and, for diagnostics, the turn of the attacking side in which each unit's wounds run out). A group's
    members lead with its target, so their volleys are pooled on it from each member's arrival: the target falls at the
    group's combined pace, and each member starts on its next target in that turn (D-067's "what a volley has left goes on
    to the next one"). That is the brief's "the group members' later targets start earlier", with no new rule.
  - **The combined pace into P(wiped): each member fights its slice of the target.** For a group on target t: M is the
    members whose queue starts with t and that take wounds from it in the timeline (dealt > 0). With two or more:
    - each member's share s_m = dealt_m / (the sum over M);
    - member m's duel is with its slice of t: t at w_t x s_m wounds (w_t its wounds now; at least 1, read between the
      whole wounds below and above it, as the chain's wounds are whole), from now (delay 0), at the pair's real position
      and stage (pair_node with a `Slice`: the chain at the slice's wounds; m's shooting in the half-turn in progress takes
      off the slice what it would take off the whole unit; t's own shooting in progress lands with probability s_m), or
      the charged pair's fight (pair_charge) when m is charge_values' charger;
    - P_G(t wiped) = the sum over M of s_m x P(m wipes its slice): the expected share of t the group destroys, each slice
      counted whole or not at all;
    - survive(t) = (1 - P_G) x the product over every other attacker's duel with t (D-067), in place of the members' own
      duels with the whole t. A member outside M (it deals nothing to t in the timeline, or it is engaged elsewhere) keeps
      its own duel.
    - Why slices: a member alone removes its slice at its own pace in w_t s_m / pace_m, which is the group's combined
      time, so each slice duel is the chain's duel at the group's pace, with its approach, the target's reply scaled down
      with the slice and every stage of the half-turn in progress, at no new table. The shares come from the timeline, so
      a member that arrives late gets a small share and the one in front a large one: a lone member in reach before its
      partners is valued nearly alone (no hiding behind partners, the batch's risk), and the group's value rises only when
      its members arrive together (M80's synchronisation, in the army value).
    - The brief's "for a group on one target, the target's wipe turn comes from the group's combined pace": the wipe turn is
      the timeline's (pooled), and P(wiped) follows it through the slices; D-067's fates took P(wiped) from independent
      duels of single attackers with the whole unit, so three monsters on the Chaos Terminators, which the timeline already
      had wiping them out in battle round 3, gave P(wiped) 0.04.
