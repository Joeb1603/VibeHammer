# VibeHammer Development Plan

Goal for V1: a Windows `.exe` where two people play Intercessor Squad (5) vs Termagants (10) hotseat on a
24"x24" board, through the full turn sequence (Command, Movement, Shooting, Charge, Fight, End of Turn),
following the 11th edition core rules. The rules come from `docs/rules/` and the units from `data/`.

Architecture, conventions and rules-lookup policy are in `CLAUDE.md`. This file is the order of work.

## How to run each milestone

1. `/clear`, then set the effort level shown for the milestone (e.g. `/effort high`).
2. If the milestone says **Plan mode**, enter plan mode (Shift+Tab) and paste the prompt. Review the design before approving.
3. Paste the prompt. Claude reads the listed rule files first.
4. The milestone is done when every acceptance check passes and `ctest` is green. Then ask Claude to commit.

### Effort levels

| Level | Use for |
|---|---|
| `medium` | Scaffolding, wiring, UI layout, data plumbing: well-specified work with few rule subtleties. |
| `high` | Ordinary rules implementation: one rule area with clear text and good test coverage. |
| `xhigh` | Rule areas with many interacting clauses, "if possible" constraints, or alternating-player flow. |
| `max` | Core architecture decisions that everything else builds on. Use it sparingly. |

If your Claude Code version doesn't have `/effort`, put "think hard" (for high) or "ultrathink" (for xhigh/max) in the prompt.

Give every type JSON round-trip support and equality. Don't add game flow yet.

**Acceptance**
- Loading the scenario gives 5 Intercessors (32 mm bases, M6 T5 Sv3+ W2 Ld6+ OC2) and 10 Termagants (28.5 mm, M6 T3 Sv5+ W1 Ld8+ OC2) with the right weapons.

- `GameState` → JSON → `GameState` round-trips equal. Tests cover unknown weapon abilities, which should load as `Unknown{text}` and not throw.
- The loaded formation matches D-008: Intercessors in one line of 5, Termagants in two ranks of 5, 1" gaps, front ranks touching their zone fronts (front lines exactly 12" apart). - Strangleweb (skill `"N/A"`, A `"D6"`) and Termagant spinefist (`"Pistol"`) load without error.

## M9: Charge phase ✅ (done)
**Effort:** `xhigh` · **Plan mode:** no
**Rules:** 11.01–11.04, 24.13

> Read rule 11. Implement charge eligibility (unengaged, within 12" of an enemy, didn't advance or fall back), target selection, the 2D6 charge roll, and the charge move with its constraints: end closer to a target, end engaged with all selected targets and no other enemy units, coherency, distance ≤ roll. Mark the "within 1" if possible / engaged if possible" clauses with `TODO(if-possible)` per CLAUDE.md. A successful charge grants Fights First until end of turn. A failed charge leaves the unit where it was.

**Acceptance**
- Charge targets must be within 12" **and within the charge roll** (11.04 Before Moving), measured edge-to-edge. That is a stricter test than whether the unit can reach engagement, which needs only (distance − 2"). Tests:
  - An enemy at 11.9" with a roll of 11: no charge target can be selected, so the charge fails and the models don't move.
  - An enemy at 9.9" with a roll of 9: the target can't be selected either, even though a 9" move would reach within 2". This guards against the 10th-edition reach check.
  - An enemy at 9.9" with a roll of 10: the target is selectable and the charge succeeds.
  - A roll of 2 never succeeds (11 sidebar "Failed charges").
- A charge that would end engaged with a non-target enemy unit is rejected (add a second Termagant unit in the test scenario for this).

## M10: Fight phase ✅ (done)
**Effort:** `xhigh` · **Plan mode:** yes
**Rules:** 12.01–12.09, 04.02 (splitting melee attacks), Appendix "Passing in the Fight Phase"

> Read rule 12 and the Rules Appendix. Plan the alternation flow first. Implement pile-in (3", models in base contact can't move, end closer to the closest pile-in target, end engaged), the Fight step with the Fights First Combats and Remaining Combats sub-steps alternating as written, Normal Fight vs Overrun Fight (extra 3" pile-in), melee weapon selection (one per model, splitting attacks across engaged targets; resolved through `begin_attacks`), and consolidation with the three priority modes (Ongoing, Engaging, Objective; V1 has no objectives). A unit that meets no mode's conditions can't consolidate (09 sidebar "Selecting modes"). An Engaging Consolidation that ends engaged with enemy units that haven't been selected to fight forces the opponent to select each of them to fight (12.08 After Moving). The flow must support fights after the Fight step has ended. Implement fight-phase passing from the appendix ("Eligible to fight, but unable to fight"). A player may pass only if all of their eligible units are more than 5" from all enemy units. The Fight step ends when both players pass in succession, or when one passes and the opponent has no eligible units. Per the appendix FAQ, an Overrun Fight requires the unit to still be eligible to fight.

**Acceptance**
- After a successful Intercessor charge, the Intercessors fight first. The alternation then continues per 12.04, which a test asserts step by step.
- A unit that is no longer engaged when selected makes an Overrun Fight.
- Consolidation picks the mandatory mode by priority, and a test covers each reachable mode.
- An Engaging Consolidation into an enemy unit that hasn't fought makes that unit fight (12.08), and a test asserts the forced selection.
- Passing is rejected while an eligible unit is within 5" of an enemy, and accepted otherwise.

> - A new headless library in `src/ai/` (`wh40k_ai`, namespace `wh40k::ai`).

> - `bot.hpp`:
>   - `enum class BotLevel { Easy, Normal, Hard }`, `struct BotConfig`;
>   - `struct BotChoice { core::GameAction action; std::string reason; }`;
>   - `class Bot { BotChoice decide(const core::GameState&, core::Side); }`. It is a stateless policy plus an
>     optional plan memo (D-054).
> - `state_hash.*`: FNV-1a over unit positions, wounds, flags, flow and pending. It excludes `state.dice`. It is used
>   for the bot's own RNG seed and the plan memo.

>   moved in unchanged as level Easy; the old test keeps passing, through the moved code if convenient.
> - `fallback.*`: a legal answer for every one of the 15 decision kinds, and the loop guard (the same state hash and
>   decision three times → fallback; fallback rejected → the bot stops and reports the reasons).
> - `bot_driver.*`: raylib-free pacing.
>   - `tick(double now)` returns an action when it's due.
>   - Thinking pauses per action kind, scaled by speed. Normal speed: 0.8 s before a unit's activation, 0.12 s
>     between model placements, 0.4 s before a commit, 0.6 s for reactive allocation and casualty answers.
>   - Slow ×2 (×3 since M48, D-059), Fast ×0.4, Instant 0.
>   - It runs `decide` on a worker `std::thread` with a `GameState` copy. A generation counter drops results that

> - `arena.*` and `arena_main.cpp` (exe `wh40k_bot_arena`, run in Release):

> - Auto actions: `AutoSettings` gets the bot sides, so auto-allocate never answers a bot's decision.

> - Input gating: every board click, drag and panel button checks that a human controls the deciding side.
> - Panel: while the bot decides it shows "Bot (Tyranids) is thinking… — <reason>", with **Pause** and **Step**.
> - Dice: a bot's own dice cards auto-roll and auto-continue after a dwell (about 1.2 s at Normal speed; a click

> - CLI: `--bot A|B|both|none`, `--bot-speed slow|normal|fast|instant`, `--bot-level easy|normal|hard`, and

## M44: Game-aware evaluator, lookahead, tuning (level Hard) ✅ (done, with open items)
**Effort:** `xhigh` · **Plan mode:** pre-approved (2026-11-06)

> **Evaluator:**
> - A small Markov chain over (models left on each side, battle round, range band) for the remaining half-turns.
> - It gives V = P(win) − P(loss), with a draw worth 0 (D-007: wipe-out wins, round 5 is a draw).
> - The winning side pushes to wipe the enemy out, and a losing side plays for the draw (kiting, staying out of
>   charge range).
> - Movement, casualty order, coherency removal and consolidation all use V.
>
> **Lookahead:** the top 3 move plans are checked by an engine-backed Monte Carlo of the rest of our turn and the
> enemy's reply. It uses the simulator with the bot's own dice, a cheap rollout policy and a fixed work budget
> (simulated actions, never wall-clock time).
>
> **Tuning:**
> - Weights live in a `BotWeights` struct, tuned with arena runs; the final values and results are recorded in
>   D-054.
> - New scenarios so the tuning isn't done on one matchup: `data/scenarios/bot_termagants_first.json` and
